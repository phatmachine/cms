## Dropped for lack of source

- **Amazon's retail and advertising conduct.** Held deliberately for issue 011 so the two pages do not overlap. The FTC ad-auction case and the ~$70bn ad business appear only there.
- **Prime membership numbers beyond Amazon's own 2021 figure.** Several third-party estimates exist; none is authoritative.
- **"Amazon admitted using dark patterns."** It did not. It settled while neither admitting nor denying. Reason 02 is worded to rest on the court's summary judgment finding instead, which is solid.
- **Prime Day pricing practices and the "raising prices before discounting" claim.** Frequently alleged, not researched here.
- **Delivery driver and warehouse conditions.** Same position as issue 011: squarely relevant to the people-over-profit principle, not researched, not printed.

## Weak link — fix before publishing

- **The "Iliad" name and the ROSCA finding should both be cited to the FTC complaint and the court's order**, not to settlement-explainer sites. Both documents are public. This page makes a strong accusation about intent and deserves primary sourcing more than any issue since 005.
- **The refund window in exit route step one.** Automatic payments ran to December 2025 and claimants had 180 days from notification. That window has probably closed by now. Check, and reword if so — telling readers to claim a refund that has expired would be the most embarrassing error available here.
- **The July 2025 "benefit modification" ruling** is sourced to PPC Land citing Variety. Verify.
- **MUBI's pricing.** Aggregator-sourced again.

## Series note — the alternatives here are the weakest fit so far

Prime is a bundle of delivery, video, music, photo storage, reading and games. MUBI covers film honestly. PeerTube covers stored video, which is a real but minor part of what people lose. Neither touches delivery, and the intro says so plainly rather than pretending.

The better long-term answer is to revisit this page once the Spotify and Audible issues have established verified music and audiobook alternatives, which are the natural Prime substitutes. Recommend flagging this page for a second pass rather than treating it as finished.

## Goes stale

- **Prime Video pricing.** Changed twice in twenty-six months and the ad-free tier has already been renamed once. Re-check every six months.
- **The refund process.** Time-limited and probably now expired.
- **The settlement's injunctive relief.** Amazon is required to keep cancellation simple. If compliance slips, that is a story in itself and would strengthen this page considerably.
- **MUBI's independence.** Newly funded at a billion-dollar valuation by Sequoia. The same pattern flagged on Bluesky and Back Market: money in, founder pressure up, exit expected eventually.
