## Dropped for lack of source or fit

- **Any claim that the Israeli government, military or intelligence services use Waze for surveillance.** Not printed, in any form. Snopes rated the claim "Mixture" on 15 July 2026: the Israeli governing law and Tel Aviv courts are real and have been in the terms since 2008; the founders reportedly served in the Israeli military; but "no credible public evidence confirms or refutes claims that Israel's government or military secretly uses Waze for user surveillance", and Google called the allegations "completely false". (The Snopes page returned 402; the finding is from a Yahoo syndication of it, https://tech.yahoo.com/social-media/articles/fact-check-inspecting-claim-israel-100000411.html.) This fails the series' documentary standard for the government-interest principle, so the page states the jurisdiction as a legal fact and stops.
- **The founders' nationality or military service.** True of the founders as reported, but biographical, not conduct, and an inference-by-association that a hostile reader would use against the page.
- **The 2016 UC Santa Barbara "ghost driver" research** (Ben Zhao and colleagues): showed that a researcher-controlled fake device could track nearby drivers in real time through Waze's protocol. Waze said it was overblown. A Threatpost article in 2020 reported a related tracking issue. I did not establish whether either was fixed, which makes it a stale-scandal risk. Not printed.
- **Law-enforcement concerns about Waze's police-reporting feature** (National Sheriffs' Association, 2014 onward). This is a dispute about officers being tracked, not about the user's privacy.
- **Waze for Cities / Connected Citizens.** Waze shares public incident and road-closure reports with city and government partners (launched 2014 with ten partners including the NYPD, Tel Aviv, Boston and Florida). Reported to include only public alerts and no individual locations. The policy also says "Cities receive aggregated traffic data". A fair point for the source ledger, but not a privacy harm on its own terms, and putting it on the page would suggest more than the sources show.
- **Waze's EU "Very Large Online Platform" status** (50.5m average monthly EU users, July–December 2024, per a Google filing). Real but about content moderation, not privacy.
- **The 1 August 2026 Terms and Privacy update.** Waze's help page announces changes effective 1 August 2026 (age 16+, advertising and data-use wording, EEA terms) but the policy and terms pages I fetched say "last modified 8 July 2026". Either the pages lag the notice or the update is within them. **Re-read both before publishing; every quotation on the page is from the 8 July 2026 text.**
- **Iraq's reported ban on Waze over "Israeli links"** (7 October 2026). Political, not privacy, and not documentary evidence of anything about the product.
- **Regulator enforcement.** I searched for a fine, finding or investigation of Waze's privacy practices and found none. The page therefore rests on Waze's own policy, not on any regulator's finding, and says nothing implying otherwise.
- **HERE WeGo, TomTom, Organic Maps, OsmAnd.** Considered. HERE and TomTom are commercial location-data businesses and their apps' data practices were not read. Organic Maps is the project CoMaps forked from over governance concerns; OsmAnd has no live traffic.
