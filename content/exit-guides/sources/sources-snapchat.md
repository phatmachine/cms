## Dropped for lack of source or fit

- **The word "evil", and any statement that Snap built its product to harm children.** Not printed. It is a moral judgement about motive, which cannot be evidenced and which the defensible-content rules do not allow as an assertion. The page instead carries the editor's view in first person ("I hate being asked to parent from a list of names"; "I hate what they describe"), each directly after the facts it rests on. That is honest opinion on disclosed facts and does the same work.
- **Signal** (earlier slot 1). Ruled out by the editor: no parental controls, disappearing messages available.
- **Threema** (earlier slot 2). Same objection; also changed ownership in January 2026.
- **Pinwheel** (kids' phone with a Safelist and parent-readable texts, including deleted ones). Researched and held back: a new phone plus $17.99 a month for the parent portal (phones $349–$499; Pinwheel Wireless $29.99 a month); reviewers say it is too restrictive for 13+; its privacy policy (updated 29 August 2025, read via summary) says US data is stored on AWS and lists Google, Meta and Klaviyo among providers for analytics and marketing, while stating it does not sell or share personal information as the CPRA defines it. Founder-led, about $2.65m raised from angel and crowdfunding investors (CB Insights summary). It is the strongest option for a parent who wants full visibility of a younger child's texts, and could replace Kinzoo if the editor prefers.
- **Netsafe (New Zealand).** Its Snapchat Family Toolkit was developed in partnership with Snapchat. A resource co-produced with the company cannot be offered as the independent counterweight, whatever its quality.
- **eSafety Commissioner (Australia), Internet Matters (UK).** Both publish Snapchat guides. The eSafety fetch timed out and neither was read. Either could take slot 1 in a market-specific version. Internet Matters is industry-funded, which would need disclosing.
- **WhatsApp teen parental controls** (reported October 2026) and **Messenger Kids.** Meta products, so they fail hard gate 1.
- **Snap Map, Quick Add, streaks.** Named in the Common Sense guide and the New Mexico complaint, but not independently sourced to Snap's documentation. Not printed.
- **Specific harms to individual children, grooming and sextortion mechanics.** Documented in lawsuits and press, deliberately left out under the skill's child-safety rule.
- **The Memories storage change.** The earlier draft led with it. It was verified accurate (archiving from January 2027; no deletion; $1.99 a month in the US) but it is a money story, and the editor asked for a safety one.
- **The shared "US-owned servers / US law" reason.** The earlier guides use it as reason 03. Replaced here by the court record because the editor wanted the safety case at the front.
