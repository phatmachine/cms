# Leaving You — TikTok US

Status: Approved by Mark with his edit to the ownership paragraph, 5 October 2026. One claim to confirm against policy text before publishing (see ledger).

---

I'm leaving you.

Not because you didn't get me — you got me faster than anyone ever has.

You know what makes me laugh, what makes me cry, what makes me stop scrolling for half a second longer than I meant to. You knew I was sad before I did. You know the hour I finally put the phone down, and the hour I picked it back up.

I came for a laugh. You learned me, swipe by swipe, until whole evenings disappeared and I couldn't say where they went.

Then you were sold. New owners, a new company, new terms I had to accept or lose everything I'd made. I was told the change would keep me safe. But the machine stayed: your algorithm was licensed to the new owners and retrained on my data. Your new privacy policy says you may now collect my precise location, which the old one said you didn't. And the promise to tell me before handing my data to law enforcement disappeared.

The owners changed. The hold didn't.

You make money from what I post, while deciding who gets to see it. How do I trust a machine whose job is to make money from me?

You were never just an app. You're a joint venture of investment firms and tech companies, with the ability to define the narrative and write your own story into the minds of millions.

But I'm done giving my evenings to a feed I can't see inside. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done making content for a machine that decides who gets heard.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Feeds that end. Creators I follow because I chose them. Places that show me what my friends posted, in the order they posted it.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.
