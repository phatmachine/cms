## A note on replacing FreeTube with CuriosityStream

FreeTube was removed on editorial instruction: it fetches video outside YouTube's terms of service, and the series will not recommend something that could be read as promoting a service that breaks the losing provider's terms. That is a positioning decision rather than a fact question, and nothing in the research contradicts it.

What went in its place is **not a like-for-like swap**, and this ledger should say so plainly rather than let a future editor assume it was:

1. **CuriosityStream is not a YouTube alternative.** It is a documentary subscription. The page now states that in the catch instead of implying a substitution that does not exist, and three fields that had assumed a YouTube-compatible player were rewritten to match: `alternatives.intro`, `exitRoute.03.body` (which previously claimed "every alternative player can import it" — false for both remaining alternatives) and `exitRoute.05.body`.
2. **The two alternatives now share a shareholder.** CuriosityStream owns 16.875% of Nebula, and its $9.99 bundle includes Nebula outright. `references/criteria.md` asks for two alternatives "genuinely different from each other"; these are different products but not independent companies. Both blocks disclose the link. **The clean fix is an unrelated third party in one of the two slots — flagged for the editor, not actioned.**
3. **The majority of CuriosityStream's revenue is now AI data licensing.** On a page arguing that a model reads your viewing history, the recommended first move is a company whose largest business is selling video to train models. It sells its own licensed footage rather than the reader's behaviour, so it is not hypocrisy — but it is the most quotable objection available to a hostile reader, which is exactly why it is disclosed in the catch rather than left for them to find.
4. **`exitRoute.04` lost its original point.** It used to end "Watching without ads while paying nobody is not a principled position" — a line aimed squarely at FreeTube's ad-blocking. With both alternatives now paid and ad-free the jab no longer lands, so the step was rewritten to keep the beat (pay two creators directly) without the dead reference.

## Dropped for lack of source

- **The 2019 FTC COPPA settlement** ($170m over children's data). Seven years old and remediated. Stale-scandal trap.
- **Radicalisation and the recommendation algorithm.** Heavily studied, genuinely contested, and impossible to state fairly in two sentences. Would need its own issue and a much deeper research pass.
- **The ad-block crackdown.** Real and irritating, but it is a fight about ad-blocking rather than about the reader's data. **With FreeTube gone the conflict of interest goes with it — this is now available as a reason if reason 03 is ever replaced.**
- **"Including Google's Gemini" as an AI licensing customer.** Repeated across trade coverage summarising a CEO interview, and it would have been the sharpest line on the page: leaving a Google product for a Google supplier. **The CEO's own transcript names no partner, and neither do the SEC filings.** Not printable on a secondary paraphrase, at any strength of hedge. Revisit only if a filing or an on-record statement names them.
- **CuriosityStream's ad-network data sharing.** Its privacy policy (last updated 19 March 2024) says it works with "advertising and analytics partners, ad networks, and social media sites", that these partnerships "may be considered a 'sale' or 'sharing' under some U.S. laws, even when no money exchanges hands", and that what may be shared includes "online activity information ... and other personal information or sensitive personal information" (https://curiositystream.com/privacy). Sourced, quotable and deliberately **not printed**: it covers marketing the service rather than the viewing inside it, and printing it beside YouTube's profiling would imply an equivalence the evidence does not support. **It belongs in any future re-ranking of this slot.**
- **YouTube Premium pricing.** Not researched this session.
- **Watch history feeding the wider Google advertising profile.** Almost certainly true and would tie neatly to issues 001 and 007, but no document was found this session stating it plainly. Reason 03 makes a narrower, sourced point instead. **Worth chasing — it would be a stronger third reason.**
- **AI slop and synthetic channels.** The parallel to issue 016 is tempting; the facts were not researched.
