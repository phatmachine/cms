## Read this before signing anything

**A referral programme is not an affiliate programme, and the difference is a
terms-of-service problem.** Refer-a-friend schemes pay the *user* in credit and
are written for personal use. Several here explicitly forbid commercial use:

- **MUBI**: referrals "should only be used for personal and non-commercial purposes".
- **Tuta**: the referral programme "is only meant for inviting people you know",
  and Tuta "may terminate contracts with people who spam or bulk mail their
  referral links". Tuta's *affiliate* programme is the commercial route.
- **Vinted**: invite-a-friend pays the user store vouchers; there is no creator
  programme at all.

Putting a personal referral code on a monetised public page can get the account
closed and would be a bad look on a site whose argument is that companies should
deal straight with people. Where both exist, take the affiliate programme.

**Disclosure is not optional.** Paid links need to be disclosed to readers —
Fair Trading Act 1986 here in New Zealand, FTC endorsement guides for US
readers, CAP/ASA rules for UK readers. The good news is the technical half is
already done: `referralUrl` renders with `rel="noopener sponsored"`, which is
the correct markup. The editorial half is a visible, plain-English line on the
page.

**The house rule this cuts against.** `rtm-exit-guide/references/criteria.md`
says the mandatory catch is "the difference between the series and an affiliate
blog, and readers can tell instantly". Taking commission does not break that —
hiding it would. Two guardrails worth adopting now, before any money arrives:

1. **Commission never decides the ranking.** The alternative that wins slot one
   wins on the eight principles. If the better option pays nothing — as is true
   for Mastodon, Bluesky, PeerTube and ARTE — it still leads.
2. **Never drop the catch to protect a commission.** The catch is what the
   reader trusts.

## Regional caveat

MUBI, Qobuz and Bolt all resolved to New Zealand storefronts. Affiliate
programmes are frequently run per-region, with separate Awin/Partnerize
programmes for US, UK and EU and no NZ programme at all. **Check NZ eligibility
and payout currency before committing to any of them** — an approved US
programme may not pay on NZ-originated traffic, and most readers of this series
are unlikely to be in one single market anyway.

## What to chase first

1. **Proton.** Three guides, verified terms, recurring revenue, and a company
   whose values match the series. Clearly the best fit.
2. **CuriosityStream.** Slot one on the YouTube guide, but apply before quoting
   any rate — the public numbers are unusable.
3. **Libro.fm, Wise, GOG, Back Market, Qobuz.** One guide each, real programmes,
   straightforward applications.
4. **Bolt for Business**, if the €150 per business sign-up is reachable from a
   consumer guide — likely not, but it is the largest single figure here.

Ten of the twenty-six will never pay anything, and they include several of the
strongest recommendations in the series. That is worth stating plainly on the
site at some point: the guides that make no money are the ones that prove the
rest are honest.
