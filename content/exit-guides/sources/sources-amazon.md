## Dropped for lack of source

- **"Amazon is a monopoly."** The FTC and 17 states sued in 2023 alleging monopoly maintenance; trial is set for 2027. Nothing is adjudicated, and a page asserting monopoly would be wrong. Not used.
- **The Prime dark-patterns case.** Amazon settled the FTC's Prime enrolment and cancellation case. That material belongs to issue 012 and is deliberately held back so the two pages do not duplicate.
- **"A quarter of Amazon search results are unlabelled ads."** From a 2021 advocacy complaint based on 130,000 search results. Five years old, Amazon disputed it, and the sponsored labelling has changed since. Not used — reason 01 makes the point from the ad revenue instead, which Amazon itself reports.
- **Alexa and Ring surveillance.** Real, previously penalised by the FTC, and genuinely alarming. Left out because this guide is about the shop; folding in voice and doorbell surveillance would make the page about a different product. Worth its own issue.
- **Working conditions in fulfilment centres.** Squarely within the "do they do good" principle and probably the reason many readers dislike Amazon most. Not researched this session, so not printed. A serious future revision should cover it properly rather than gesture at it.

## Weak link — fix before publishing

- **`subject.users` = 200 Million.** This is the weakest metadata figure in eleven issues. Amazon does not publish an active-customer count, and 200m is a five-year-old Prime figure, which is the wrong measure for a page about the shop. Options: relabel the field for this issue, use a dated third-party estimate and say so, or use Amazon's reported customer-account figure if a current one can be found. Do not publish as-is without a decision.
- **Reason 03 should cite Amazon's live privacy notice**, not a third-party policy archive. The archive has SHA-256 snapshots and cites the notice as updated 29 April 2026, which is unusually good provenance for a secondary source, but this is Amazon's own document and it is public.
- **Reason 02 is an allegation** and the page says so in terms. Keep it that way. If a future edit tightens the wording, the words "alleged", "rejects" and "nothing is proven" must survive. Check the docket before publishing in case of early dismissal.
- **Back Market's Plus pricing** comes from a subscription aggregator, not Back Market. Confirm.
- **Vinted's profitability claim** — soften to 2025 unless the earlier years are verified.

## Goes stale

- **The FTC ad case.** Filed two weeks ago. Expect motions, and possibly dismissal. Re-check monthly; this is the fastest-moving claim on the page.
- **The FTC monopoly case**, trial listed for 2027. If Amazon is found liable, this page gains a much stronger reason. If it wins, any future monopoly framing is closed off.
- **Amazon's ad revenue**, reported quarterly. Re-check annually.
- **Back Market's leadership.** Founder Thibaud Hug de Larauze handed the chief executive role to Clément Petit on 1 September 2026, staying as executive chairman. A founder stepping back at a heavily funded company is the pattern flagged on Bluesky in issue 009. Watch it.
- **Vinted's ownership.** Profitable, growing and privately held by VCs who will eventually want an exit. Re-check at republication.

## Series note — Amazon Hosting

Still recommended for removal from the exit-guide format, as flagged when the backlog was set. A reader cannot leave AWS; they have no account and no exit route. The exposure is real but it is not theirs to act on, and the five-step template would produce a page that insults them. It is a strong issue page — *the websites you use all live in one company's buildings* — and should run as one.
