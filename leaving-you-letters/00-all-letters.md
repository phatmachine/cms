## Leaving You — Gmail

I'm leaving you.

Not because you don't work — you work brilliantly.

You know everything about me: what I search at 2 a.m. when I can't sleep, who I email when I'm angry, where I drive when I need time to think, what I watch when I'm alone. You've kept my life in Photos, my private thoughts in Docs, my arguments in Gmail, and a map of everywhere I've been for years.

I gave you my life because you made it easy. You gave me tools that just worked. But somewhere along the way, things changed. You became about power and control.

You built an empire on my data, my patterns, my intimate moments. You're in my pocket, on my wrist, in my living room, and at my school.

Then the record caught up with you. State after state said you kept tracking where I went after I'd switched it off — and you paid well over a billion dollars to settle. Two federal judges ruled you broke the law to protect your monopolies: first in search, then in advertising.

I wove you so deeply into my daily life that leaving feels almost impossible. But I'm done funding your profit with my privacy. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done being the product you sell.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Email encrypted so even the provider can't read it. Cloud storage that can't see inside my files. Maps that don't keep a record of where I've been. Companies that answer to me, not to advertisers.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.

## Leaving You — Audible

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.

## Leaving You — Canva

I'm leaving you.

Not because you weren't easy — you made me feel like a designer.

You hold my birthday invitations, my business cards, the flyer for the school fair, the pitch deck that won me my first client. You know the fonts I always reach for and the templates I keep coming back to.

I came because I couldn't afford a designer. Then I stopped thinking I needed one. So did everyone else.

Then the posters started to look the same. Same layouts, same fonts, same smiling stock faces, from the café on my street to the notice on the lamppost. The local designer who used to make our town look like *us* lost the work, one template at a time.

And once I depended on you, the price changed. You raised team prices by up to several times over, pointing to new AI features I hadn't asked for. You bought an AI image company, and the rival design tools the professionals used.

You were never just a design app. You're a company building AI to design for me, and calling it creativity.

But I'm done squeezing my ideas into someone else's template. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done letting a machine decide what my town looks like.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Free, open design tools I can keep using whatever happens. Files I can open anywhere. Local designers, paid to make things that look like us.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.
