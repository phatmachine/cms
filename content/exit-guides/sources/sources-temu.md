## Dropped for lack of source

- **"Temu is spyware."** The claim traces largely to a 2023 short-seller report (Grizzly Research) and to a separate malware finding about Pinduoduo's Chinese app, which is a different product. Neither was verified here, and the short-seller had a financial position. Not printed. The Commission's product-safety findings are stronger, adjudicated, and about something the reader can hold in their hand.
- **Forced labour and UFLPA allegations.** Raised by a US congressional committee in 2023. Serious, politically contested, and not researched this session. If the series covers it, it needs a dedicated pass and careful sourcing — not a line borrowed from a committee press release.
- **Data collection and app permissions.** No current primary source found. Notably, the page makes no privacy claim at all, which is unusual for this series and deliberate: the documented harm here is physical product safety.
- **The €3 EU parcel fee and the French fast-fashion law.** Both appeared in search results as recent developments affecting Temu. Neither was researched properly. Both are worth chasing for a revision — they change the price argument directly.
- **Temu's response to the fine.** Temu said earlier it would "continue to cooperate fully with the Commission". Its response to the final decision was not established. Find it; the page should note any appeal.

## Weak link — fix before publishing

- **`subject.users`.** A DSA threshold is a floor, not a count. Temu publishes an EU user figure in its DSA transparency reporting. Get it.
- **The Refurbed workforce-cut line.** Sourced to an undated analyst summary on a jobs board. Either verify it against reporting or cut that sentence — the warranty point stands on its own, and a wrong claim about a company's health is exactly the kind of error that damages the alternative rather than the subject.
- **The August 2026 action plan deadline** has passed. Find out what happened before this publishes, or the page reads as out of date on its central enforcement story.
- **Cite the Commission's own decision**, not law-firm and monitoring-service summaries. The preliminary findings release is already public at digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu; the May 2026 release should be too.

## Series note — the alternatives are starting to repeat

This is the second consecutive shopping issue recommending a refurbished-electronics marketplace and Vinted. Back Market (issue 011) and Refurbed are near-identical propositions in different countries, and Vinted now appears twice.

That is a real finding rather than laziness: the honest field of European, non-ad-funded consumer marketplaces is small. Two options, as with the AI cluster in issues 006–008: say so openly on the archive page, or accept that the shopping issues will converge and consider merging them. Do not solve it by promoting a worse alternative for variety's sake.

## Goes stale

- **The DSA enforcement.** Fastest-moving item. The other strands of the October 2024 proceedings — addictive design, recommender systems, researcher access — could produce further fines. Re-check quarterly.
- **Import rules.** The EU's €3 low-value parcel fee and national fast-fashion laws directly affect Temu's pricing. If delivered prices rise, the alternatives' cost gap narrows and the page's argument gets easier.
- **PDD's corporate presentation.** The Dublin/Shanghai ambiguity is itself a moving target and central to reason 03. Re-check at each republication.
- **Refurbed's health.** Newly funded, recently profitable, reportedly cutting staff. Confirm before republishing.
