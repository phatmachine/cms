## Dropped for lack of source

- **"God View."** Uber's internal tool for tracking riders, exposed in 2014 and settled with the New York AG in 2016. Twelve years old, remediated, and the textbook stale-scandal trap.
- **Worker classification and the gig-economy litigation.** The UK Supreme Court ruled in 2021 that Uber drivers are workers; similar cases run across Europe. Genuinely central to the people-over-profit principle and probably deserves a reason, but not researched this session. The algorithmic-management fine covers adjacent ground with better sourcing.
- **Surge and personalised pricing.** Repeatedly alleged, including claims that Uber prices by phone battery level or individual willingness to pay. Not verified, and Uber disputes the personalised-pricing framing. Not printed.
- **Uber Advertising revenue.** The model field says "Commission And Ads" on the strength of general knowledge rather than a source. Fix or change the field.
- **Safety incident data.** Uber publishes US safety reports. Not examined, and a page arguing about assault statistics would need far more care than a single research pass allows.

## Weak link — fix before publishing

- **Reason 01 rests on one secondary source.** The €825m fine is the lead of this page, it is three weeks old, and it is sourced to a single aggregator citing Reuters. Get the Reuters report and the Dutch DPA's own release. If the AP has published the decision, cite it directly. **Nothing else on this page matters until this is done.**
- **Add the appeal.** Uber says it will appeal. The page currently presents the fine without that. One clause fixes it, and its absence is the clearest fairness gap in fifteen issues.
- **The 2018 and 2023 fines** in reason 03 come from the same single source. Verify both.
- **Bolt's commission and fare claims** originate with Bolt. Verify or soften; recommending an alternative on its own marketing is exactly what this series is supposed to avoid.
- **`subject.users` is TK.**

## Series note — the user-count problem needs one dedicated pass

This is the second TK in two issues, and the underlying problem is now visible across the series:

- Issue 004 (Facebook): 2.28bn is an ad-reach figure from an aggregator, not a user count
- Issue 011 (Amazon): 200m is a five-year-old Prime figure on a page about the shop
- Issue 013 (Temu): a DSA threshold floor rather than a count
- Issues 014 and 015: TK placeholders

Recommend one dedicated metadata pass across all fifteen issues, pulling each figure from the company's own most recent disclosure and recording the measure used — monthly active users, active accounts, registered users, units shipped. These are not comparable measures and the series currently mixes them silently. That pass would also settle whether the field should be relabelled per issue.

## Goes stale

- **The €825m fine.** Under appeal, and appeals in the Netherlands are reported to take around four years. The page will need updating at each stage.
- **Bolt's profitability.** It turned a small net profit in 2025 after losing €102m in 2024. A thin margin and aggressive acquisitions; re-check annually.
- **The Drivers Cooperative's geography.** The catch says New York only. If it expands, or if a European co-op reaches scale, this recommendation gets much stronger. Re-check every six months — this is the alternative in the series most aligned with the brief and the most likely to improve.
- **Uber's regulatory exposure in the EU.** Four Dutch fines in eight years, and the algorithmic-management limb is new ground. Expect more.
