# Leaving You — Snapchat

Status: Approved by Mark, 5 October 2026. Parent voice. Publish timing is Mark's call (launch plan holds parent content until after day 90).

---

I'm leaving you — and I'm taking my child with me.

Not because they didn't love you. They did. You were where their friends were, where the jokes were, where the streaks lived that they'd never let die.

For a while, you knew my child better than I did. Their best friends. Their late nights. Their dot on a map. The messages that vanished before anyone else could see them. You sold disappearing as a feature. For some children, it was the danger.

I believed a company this big would keep them safe. Then a state took you to court, using your own documents. New Mexico's lawsuit says your staff were getting thousands of reports a month about children being threatened and exploited, and warned that this was only a fraction. It says reports slipped through the cracks, that accounts stayed open long after they were flagged, and that the people trying to fix it met resistance from above.

You say safety is built in. Your own staff, in your own words, said it wasn't enough.

And you were never just an app for kids. You're Snap: a company that earns from every minute my child spends with you.

I let you into my child's pocket because everyone else had. Leaving feels like cutting them off from their friends. But I'm done handing my child to a place I can't see into. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done being told it's safe by the people who were warned it wasn't.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Messaging where my child only talks to people they actually know. Tools that let a family stay close without spying. Friendships with faces, at the park and at the front door.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for us to leave.
