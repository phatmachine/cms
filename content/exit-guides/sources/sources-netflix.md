## Dropped for lack of source

- **Any privacy or data claim.** None found that meets this series' bar. Netflix collects viewing data and now runs its own ad technology, but no regulator has found against it and no document was located describing targeting from viewing history in the way issues 001 or 006 could. The absence is the finding, and the page says so implicitly by arguing about something else.
- **Titles disappearing from the catalogue.** True of all streaming including both alternatives, so it would be a dishonest reason. Cut.
- **Ad-tier title blocking.** Was a real complaint: 326 of 6,324 titles blocked at launch in November 2022. As of July 2026 it is 38 of 8,306, or 0.46%. **The problem has been largely fixed, so using it would be the stale-scandal trap.** Cut, and noted here so nobody reinstates it.
- **The 2023 password-sharing crackdown.** Three years old, thoroughly relitigated, and commercially successful. Not a live argument.
- **The "sophomore slump" retention narrative.** Netflix disputes it and it is a business-performance story, not a reader-facing one.

## Weak link — fix before publishing

1. **Add Netflix's stated reason to reason 01.** Third consecutive issue with this gap. Netflix says the change keeps focus on revenue and operating profit and that it will still publish title-level view hours and weekly Top 10s. The reason survives that context easily, and omitting it is starting to look like a pattern rather than an oversight.
2. **Localise reason 03.** The $17.99 figure is US pricing on a page aimed at British and European readers.
3. **Confirm ARTE's corporate structure on arte.tv**, not a subscription aggregator, and verify the no-account claim.
4. **Confirm Nebula's current pricing.**
5. **Label `subject.users` as a floor**, since Netflix no longer reports the number.

## Series note — a recurring fairness gap

Issues 015, 016 and 018 have each stated a criticism without the company's published response: Uber's appeal, Spotify's AI crackdown, Netflix's stated rationale. Each was caught in the ledger rather than on the page.

**Recommend adding an explicit step to the skill:** before a reason is finalised, check whether the company has answered publicly, and include the answer where one exists. The reasons in this series are strong enough to survive the other side being heard, and including it is what separates the page from advocacy.

## Goes stale

- **Netflix's disclosure.** The annual-only viewership report begins Q1 2027, weeks after this would publish. Reason 01's tense will need checking.
- **Pricing.** Changed in January 2025 and again since. Re-check every six months.
- **The ad business.** Targeting $3bn in 2026 and growing fast. If Netflix begins targeting ads from viewing history in a documented way, this page gains a fourth and much sharper reason.
- **ARTE's funding.** Public broadcasting budgets are politically contested in both France and Germany. Its independence is its whole value here.
- **Nebula's ownership.** CuriosityStream already holds a minority stake. If that grows, the creator-owned claim weakens and the catch becomes the headline.
