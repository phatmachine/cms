# Leaving You — Amazon

Status: Approved by Mark with his revision to the warehouse paragraph, 5 October 2026

---

I'm leaving you.

Not because you let me down — you never did. It always came tomorrow.

You know what I buy when I'm sad, what I reorder every month, what I looked at three times and didn't buy. You know my address, my door, my size, my kids' birthdays, and every gift I've sent and who it went to.

I came because you made it easy. One click. No queue, no trip, no waiting. I never had to think about how.

But the people who packed my box were paying for it. US workplace safety regulators cited you over the back injuries your warehouse work was causing. A US Senate investigation said your own studies showed that slowing down would hurt fewer people — and that you chose speed. Now your internal documents, reported by the New York Times, describe replacing hundreds of thousands of those workers with robots, and calling them "cobots" so it sounds kinder.

And you were never just a shop. You're Ring on my front door, Alexa in my kitchen, Prime Video on my TV, Audible in my ears and Whole Foods in my fridge.

I let you become my high street. Leaving feels like going back to waiting. But I'm done letting someone else's back pay for my convenience. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done buying things I didn't need just because they'd arrive tomorrow.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Local shops that know my name instead of my data. Sellers who pay the people who pack the box. Things worth waiting a few days for.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.
