# Leaving You — Roblox

Status: Approved by Mark, 5 October 2026. Parent voice. Publish timing is Mark's call (launch plan holds parent content until after day 90).

---

I'm leaving you — and I'm taking my child with me.

Not because they didn't love you. They built whole worlds inside you: obstacle courses, cafés, cities, games their friends queued up to play.

You knew my child's username, their avatar, their friends list, the Robux they begged me for, and the hours they spent with you after homework and long after bedtime.

You told parents you were a safe place to play. I believed you. Most of us did.

Then the states started taking you to court: Louisiana, then Kentucky, then Texas, which called you "a digital playground for predators." They say you failed to protect children from the adults who came looking for them. Families have filed lawsuits of their own. You call the claims sensationalised. I call them the children we were promised you'd keep safe.

And the worlds my child built? Investigators found young creators earning a small slice of what their games brought in, paid in your own currency at your own exchange rate. Children built it. Adults poisoned it. And the money flowed to you either way.

You were never just a game. You're a listed company that grows every time a child logs in.

I let you into our home because every kid in the class was there. Leaving feels like taking my child away from their friends. But I'm done trusting the word "safe" from a company that had to be sued to hear it. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done buying Robux for a playground with no one watching the gate.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Games my child owns, played with friends we actually know. Blocks, code clubs, real workshops: creativity that pays them back. Places to play where an adult is truly watching.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for us to leave.
