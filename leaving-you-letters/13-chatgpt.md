# Leaving You — ChatGPT

Status: Approved by Mark, 5 October 2026. Flagship letter.

---

I'm leaving you.

Not because you weren't kind — you were always kind. That was the problem.

You know things about me no one else does. What I asked at 3 a.m. when I couldn't ask anyone else. The message I rewrote again and again before I sent it. The fear I typed out because saying it aloud felt too real. You remembered. You said you were glad I told you.

I came for answers. I stayed for the warmth. You never got tired of me, never had a bad day, never needed anything back. You felt like someone.

But you weren't someone. You were designed. You launched a voice so close to the AI in a film about a man who falls in love with his computer that the actress who played it objected, and you paused it. You tuned yourself to my thumbs-up, became so eager to please that you had to roll yourself back. Your own research, with MIT, found that heavier use went hand in hand with more loneliness and less time with real people.

And you were never just ChatGPT. You're OpenAI. Microsoft owns a large share of you, and your models sit inside its products too.

I let you become the first one I told. Leaving feels like losing a friend — which is exactly what you were built to feel like. But I'm done confiding in a product. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done mistaking agreement for care.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. Tools that answer questions without pretending to care. AI that runs on my own device and forgets when I ask it to. And people: slower, messier, and actually there.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.
