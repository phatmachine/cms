## Dropped for lack of source

- **"Audiblegate."** The 2020–21 controversy over Audible's returns policy clawing back royalties already paid to authors. Genuinely serious and much better suited to this page than some of what is on it — but five years old, and Amazon changed the returns policy in response. Would need checking to see what the current policy is before it could run.
- **Amazon's share of the audiobook market.** Frequently cited at anything from 60% to 90%. No authoritative figure found, and it would be the natural replacement for the missing user count.
- **Audible's AI narration programmes.** Amazon runs a separate "Virtual Voice" AI narration scheme, and reporting on whether ACX accepts AI-narrated audio is contradictory: some sources say it is prohibited in standard submissions, others that select publishers are being let through. Given issue 016 ran an AI-slop reason about Spotify, the parallel is tempting — but the facts here are not settled and several sources have a commercial interest in the answer. Left out entirely.
- **Credit expiry on cancellation.** Stated in exit route step one. Long-standing Audible behaviour, not verified against current terms this session. **Verify — a reader following that step and finding it wrong is a direct harm.**

## Weak link — fix before publishing

This page has more open questions than any other in the series. In priority order:

1. **Read ACX's own terms.** Reasons 01 and 02 both describe them, from sources selling competing products. Amazon publishes them.
2. **Resolve the reason 01 / reason 02 contradiction.** If the 2026 model really does cut exclusivity to 90 days, the seven-year framing describes a system being retired, and the page would be attacking something Amazon has just fixed. That is precisely the stale-scandal trap this series exists to avoid.
3. **Source the DRM claim properly.** It is true, and currently rests on a competitor's marketing.
4. **Verify Storytel independently.** One weak source for an entire alternative.
5. **Confirm credit expiry** before telling readers to spend their credits.
6. **Fill or rethink `subject.users`.**

## Goes stale

- **The ACX royalty transition.** Legacy terms reportedly close on 31 December 2026, weeks after this would publish. Whatever the page says about royalties will need revisiting in January.
- **Libro.fm's catalogue and bookshop count.** Growing; the page avoids numbers where sources disagreed.
- **Storytel's pricing.** Varies by country and the page quotes euros to a probably-UK readership.
- **Audible's AI programmes.** Moving quickly and contradictorily reported. If ACX opens to AI narration at scale, that becomes the strongest reason on this page.
