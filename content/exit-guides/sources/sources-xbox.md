## Dropped for lack of source

- **The 2025–26 layoffs and studio closures.** Around 9,000 Microsoft layoffs, with Perfect Dark, Everwild and an unannounced Elder Scrolls Online MMO cancelled, following the $68.7bn Activision acquisition. **This was my intended third reason and it is the one that best fits the series' human-values brief.** It is dropped only because the sole source found was an aggregator (tech-insider.org). It is extensively reported elsewhere. **Research it properly and it should probably replace reason 03.**
- **Xbox executives suggesting laid-off staff use AI chatbots.** Widely circulated in late 2025. Not verified, and the kind of story that mutates in retelling.
- **Delisting and store closures** (the Xbox 360 marketplace, purchased games becoming unplayable). Strong support for reason 01 but not researched.
- **Data collection on Xbox.** No current source found, and the page makes no privacy claim — the argument here is ownership and pricing, as with issues 016 and 018.

5. **Label `subject.users`** as Game Pass members and as Microsoft's last disclosure, February 2024.

## A note on fairness

This page states Microsoft's reversal in reason 03 rather than burying it. That is deliberate, and it is the correction to the pattern flagged across issues 015, 016, 018, 019, 020 and 021, where the company's answer kept ending up in the ledger instead of the page.

It also makes the argument stronger. "They raised it 50%" invites the reply that they put it back. "They raised it 50%, lost millions of subscribers, put it back 15% above where it started and removed day-one Call of Duty as the price of doing so" does not.

**Recommend this becomes the template for the whole series**: state the criticism, state the company's response, and let the reader see that the criticism survives it.

## Goes stale

- **Game Pass pricing.** Moved twice in seven months. Cost is now the leading cancellation reason, and Microsoft is running discounted annual win-back offers. Re-check quarterly.
- **GOG's independence.** Three weeks into new ownership at the time of the last confirmation, and running on thin margins. The most consequential thing to watch here.
- **itch.io's sustainability.** One owner, no investors, a catalogue that is largely free. Admirable and fragile.
- **Microsoft Gaming's leadership.** Phil Spencer retired February 2026; Asha Sharma took over and immediately reversed a pricing decision. Further strategy changes are likely.
