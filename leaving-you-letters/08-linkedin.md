# Leaving You — LinkedIn

Status: Approved by Mark, 5 October 2026

---

I'm leaving you.

Not because you didn't help me — you did. You found me a job once. Maybe twice.

You know my whole working life: every title, every reference, every gap I tried to explain. You know who I worked with, who I lost touch with, and who looked at my profile at midnight before the interview.

I joined to stay close to the people I'd worked with. Somewhere along the way, they disappeared behind announcements. Thrilled to share. Humbled to announce. Honoured to receive. A promotion, an award, a launch, a leadership lesson learned from a toddler. Every day, everyone winning — and nobody I recognised.

Then even the winning stopped being theirs. A study estimated that more than half of the long posts on your feed were likely written with AI, and you sell the tool that writes them. You also switched on, by default, the use of my profile and posts to train your AI.

And you were never just LinkedIn. You're Microsoft: the same company behind Windows, Outlook, Teams and Copilot.

I let you become my CV, my reputation, my proof that I exist at work. Leaving feels like vanishing from my own industry. But I'm done measuring my worth in endorsements. I'm done pretending this relationship is fair. I'm done applauding a highlight reel nobody is actually living.

There are alternatives — built by people who don't think love means surveillance. A portfolio I own, on my own site. Professional communities where people talk instead of announce. References from people who actually know my work.

I've given you enough. Now, I will give you no more.

It's time for me to leave.
